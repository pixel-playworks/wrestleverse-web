## Wrestling fans get to run the show as Pixel Playworks reveals WrestleVerse 2

Pixel Playworks has revealed WrestleVerse 2, a wrestling management simulator that puts fans in charge of building the roster, shaping the rivalries and deciding who becomes the next main-event star.

Launching in 2027, the sequel takes the management appeal familiar to Football Manager players into professional wrestling, where success depends not just on assembling talent but on booking compelling shows, managing personalities and winning over the crowd.

The announcement follows the success of the original WrestleVerse, which launched in August 2025 and was a huge success with the wrestling and gaming community.

The first edition of the game holds a worldwide App Store rating of 4.8 stars and reached number three in the UK and number seven in the US App Store sports games charts.

WrestleVerse 2 builds on that foundation with new commentary and audio engines that bring players’ decisions to life through spoken commentary, crowd reactions and authentic arena sound effects. Commentary is generated instantly and works offline, removing the wait and internet connection required in the original.

Who gets the title shot? When do you pull the trigger on a shocking heel turn? And what happens when the crowd gets behind the wrestler you booked to lose?

Designed to be easy to pick up without sacrificing depth, WrestleVerse 2 gives players control over their promotion’s stories, finances and backstage relationships with interfaces built for desktop and tablet screens as well as phones.

Alongside its new audio features, the sequel will introduce managers, pay-per-appearance contracts and an in-game social media feed featuring figures from the real wrestling world. 

Fast simulation is also a development priority, keeping the focus on players’ next decisions rather than waiting for the game to advance.

The game is being developed in collaboration with Lee McAteer, former co-owner of PROGRESS Wrestling, who has booked talent including AEW Champion Will Ospreay, Swerve Strickland and Shelton Benjamin. 

McAteer’s experience brings a promoter’s perspective to the decisions players face, from building a roster to knowing when a rivalry is ready for its biggest match.

Behind WrestleVerse are James Saeed and Ethan Duckett, who met studying Computer Science at the University of Liverpool.

Saeed, who grew up in Liverpool, previously developed software at Square and trained as a professional wrestler before moving into ring announcing. 

Wrexham-born Duckett brings software engineering experience in aerospace and battery management systems alongside a longstanding enthusiasm for management games.

The project began with Saeed’s university prototype in 2018, with Duckett contributing testing and feedback during their time as housemates. Pro Wrestling Manager followed in 2021, laying the foundations for WrestleVerse’s launch in 2025.

James Saeed, CEO of Pixel Playworks, said: "The idea started with a game I wanted to play myself, a wrestling management sim with real depth that felt natural on a phone.

"Ethan and I talked about it at university, and seeing it grow into something people spend so much time playing has been incredibly rewarding.

"What excites me most about WrestleVerse 2 is hearing a show you’ve booked come together. The commentary, the crowd and the arena sound work together to make your stories feel like a wrestling show, rather than just a list of results.

"Blind and visually impaired players have told us how much it means that the original works with their screen readers so I’m especially excited for them to experience this new layer of audio. Bringing WrestleVerse to Steam for the first time is also a childhood dream come true."

Lee McAteer added: "As a promoter and booker, you can have a finish in mind for months, but you still must listen to the room. The crowd might get behind someone you thought was two years away from headlining or tell you a rivalry has run its course before you’ve reached the match you were building towards.

"I wanted to help bring that side of booking to WrestleVerse 2. It’s not about finding one right answer. It’s about making the call, seeing how people respond and working out what you do next."

WrestleVerse 2 will launch in 2027 on iOS and macOS through the App Store, Android through Google Play, and Windows and Linux through Steam.

It will be available as a one-time purchase for the full game, with no adverts, subscriptions, microtransactions or pay-to-win mechanics. Pricing will be announced at a later date. 

A free, invite-only beta is planned for summer 2027, with further information to follow in the coming months.
